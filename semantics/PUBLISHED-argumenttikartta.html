2 · Words used here

Definitions of published statement, claim, premises and the three roles

d-published · r. 25 Määritelmä D

A published statement is a parameterless Prop definition in one of five namespaces, and it states a result without proving it

A published statement is a definition of type Prop without parameters whose name has the form ….Published.X, ….Pub.X, ….Lit.X, ….LiteratureTheorems.X or ….Hypotheses₂.X. An example is GlobalTheory.Published.JacquetShalikaRigidity. Such a definition states a result and proves nothing.

d-claim · r. 30 Määritelmä D

Claim, end of a claim and premises of a theorem are read from its statement text at the first colon and the last arrow

The claim of a theorem is the part of its statement after the first colon outside brackets, and its end is the part after the last arrow outside brackets. The end of a claim without an arrow is the whole claim. The premises of a theorem are the hypotheses written before that colon and the parts of the claim before its end.

a-note-arg · r. 135 lahtokohta A

The note says the argument descends from Stepanov's and wins multiplicity through a chain of about √X theta lifts between definite unitary groups

The note is remark.pdf. It says the following about the argument.

The argument descends from the argument of Stepanov for the Riemann Hypothesis over function fields, which uses a divisor with multiplicity.

Multiplicity is hard to find over the integers, and the argument gets it from a chain of theta lifts between totally definite unitary groups, where each lift wins one more order of multiplicity.

For a von Mangoldt sum of length X the chain has about the square root of X lifts.
